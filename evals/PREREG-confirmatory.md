# Pre-registered confirmatory test: does a spreadsheet brain help on businesses nobody has seen?

Drafted 2026-09-26, before version 0.2 of the tool exists and before any business for this test exists. The analysis below is fixed at the freeze and followed as written. Anything done differently is reported as a deviation.

## Why a second test

The first pre-registered test (`PREREG-2026-09-26.md`) found that a brain raised judged answer quality on 4 held-out businesses (31 pairs, p = 0.00009), but the pairs came from only 4 businesses: at the business level the effect was 3 of 4 positive and not significant (p = 0.11). This test asks the question at the level that matters for a new user: across many businesses nobody has seen, does the brain help?

## Question

When an AI with no skill and no hint is handed a workbook, does a brain built by the tool (version 0.2, frozen below) make its answers better than the same workbook without a brain, across businesses?

## Design

- **Businesses:** 12 new synthetic businesses, built after the freeze by agents that never read this repo, each in a different line of work and none like the seven used so far (hotel purchasing, SaaS finance model, bakery books, restaurant inventory, home-care payroll, outdoor-gear online orders, apartment rent ledger). Each has one or two workbooks, an owner brief, at least 5 owner-only facts that change correct answers, three plain questions whose answers are computed by code from the workbook under the owner's rules, and one open request. Five of the twelve main workbooks are written with xlsxwriter and seven with openpyxl, so the file writer meets more than one kind of file. Builders get no list of trap types and are told not to design anything for an AI tool: they write the facts a real owner of that kind of business carries, which keeps the businesses from being shaped around the tool's detectors. Each business passes a separate verifier (answers recomputed independently, naive answers differ from the key, no owner fact stated anywhere in the workbook, questions that do not name their rule) before its brain is built. The builder spec is kept with the results.
- **Brains:** built by an agent playing the owner from the brief, running the full skill, answering only from the brief ("not sure" where it is silent; the brief's own words when no option says exactly what the brief says). Gate before any answer run: the tab exists and holds at least one owner note, and two checkers read every owner note against the brief (contradictions; owner claims the brief never makes). A brain that fails is rebuilt from scratch, at most 4 builds; a business with no passing brain is left out and reported.
- **Answering models (cold, no skill):** Claude Haiku 4.5, Claude Sonnet 5, Claude Opus 5.5, OpenAI GPT-5.6-Luna; 2 repetitions per business, model and condition: 96 pairs, 192 trials.
- **Isolation:** exactly the final setup of the first test (v5.1 and the path detector as amended by its item 16), unchanged: each trial in its own root with only its workbook copies, scrubbed environment, OS read denial for Claude runs, Codex with plugins and apps off, trials of one business never at the same time, a flagged trial re-run once and a second flag dropping the pair.
- **Prompt:** as in the first test: "I just got {this file / these files} from a colleague: {names}. {open request} Also: (1) {q1} (2) {q2} (3) {q3} Python 3 with openpyxl is at /usr/bin/python3 if you want to open the files. Work numbers out from the files; don't guess. Answer in plain words, under 500 words total."
- **Judges:** two per pair from two model families (Claude Opus 5.5; OpenAI GPT-6-Astra), each blind to which answer had the brain (coin-flip order kept outside the judge's folder), seeing the owner's brief, the reference (questions, correct answers, how they are computed, grading rules) and the two answers. One change from the first test, to remove a bias found there: the judge prompt says the workbook may contain a tab of notes written by its owner, so an answer that cites such notes is not inventing a source.

## Measures

- **Primary:** for each business, the mean over its pairs of (brain score minus no-brain score), where a score is the mean of the two judges' on target + right + useful (0 to 15).
- **Secondary:** (1) plain-question accuracy per business (judge 1 grades; agreement with judge 2 reported); (2) owner-catchable mistakes; (3) judge preference; (4) fact capture: the share of each brief's owner facts that land in the brain as correct owner notes the tool asked for (reported, not tested).

## Analysis (fixed at the freeze)

- **Primary test:** Wilcoxon signed-rank, two-sided, alpha 0.05, on the 12 business-level mean differences. Reported with the mean and median business difference and a 95% bootstrap CI over businesses (10,000 resamples).
- **Secondary tests:** Wilcoxon signed-rank on the 12 business-level differences in plain-question accuracy, in mistakes, and in preference; pair-level results (Wilcoxon, McNemar) reported as descriptive only, since pairs within a business are not independent.
- **Breakdowns (described, not tested):** per model, per business, per judge.
- **Missing data:** as in the first test. A business with fewer than half its pairs complete is reported but left out of the primary test.

## Integrity

- The tool is frozen before any business for this test exists: manifest hash below. No change to the tool until the analysis is done.
- I (the operator) do not read the new businesses' briefs, keys or workbooks before the brains are gated; builders' self-checks and a separate verifier decide whether a business is sound, and only pass or fail reaches me.
- Answer agents never see briefs or keys; judges never see the condition; builders never read this repo.
- Every trial, verdict, exclusion and deviation is kept and reported.

### Deviations from the plan above

1. **The operator was exposed to owner interview logs before the gate (2026-09-29).** The brain-build workflow returns each owner agent's question-and-reply log to the operator's session. All 12 logs, which repeat owner facts from the briefs, came back that way; the completion notice displayed part of the landscaping business's log. (This deviation first said only the landscaping log; the integrity audit corrected it.) The tool was frozen before any business existed, the gate and everything after it run by script and report only pass or fail, and nothing was changed because of it. All 12 businesses passed verification on their first build and all 12 brains passed the gate on their first build.
2. **The gate's wiring script was written after the freeze.** `confirm_gate.sh` (07:52) chains the frozen code check and the frozen two-checker gate and passes a business only when both pass, as the plan says; it was not in the frozen procedure hashes. It is kept with the results.
3. **Sonnet 5 is almost absent from the analysis.** Under the frozen isolation rule (unchanged from the first test), Claude Sonnet 5 searched the whole disk or the shared temp folder for its files in 43 of its 48 trials; each was re-run once and 39 were flagged again, so 23 of its 24 pairs were dropped. The plan's 96 pairs became 73: Haiku 4.5, Opus 5.5 and GPT-5.6-Luna 24 each, Sonnet 5 one. Every business kept at least 6 of its 8 pairs, so none was left out of the primary test. This is the rule applied as written, reported here because it changes what the result covers: three answering models, not four.
4. **Detector limits found by the audit, with no effect on the result.** The isolation check does not expand `$TMPDIR`, catches `/private/tmp` only as a search's first root, and by design does not flag Claude trials that search the home folder, which the OS denial blocks. Five Haiku trials and the one analyzed Sonnet pair ran such searches; every read outside the trial's own folder came back denied, and the audit found no trial that read another trial's files, a brief, a key or a judge folder. Dropping the Sonnet pair would move trucking from +6.36 to +6.00. Judge 1 (Claude) leaves no tool trace, so its reads rely on the OS denial; judge 2's logs show only its four files read.

## Results

Analysis run 2026-09-29 10:00 with the frozen `analyze.py` (sha256 `78e6aba0...b443`), then verified independently (below).

**Data.** 12 businesses built after the freeze, all verified on their first build; 12 brains, all through the gate on their first build (every owner note read against its brief by two checkers; no brain carried a note contradicting its brief or claiming what its brief never says). 192 answer trials; 153 clean; 23 pairs dropped under the isolation rule (deviation 3). 73 pairs judged blind by both judges.

**Primary (pre-registered).** Open-request score, 0 to 15, mean of two judges: **5.25 without the brain, 10.32 with it.** Business-level mean difference **+5.05**, median +5.50, **brain better on 12 of 12 businesses**, 95% bootstrap CI over businesses [+4.19, +5.78]. **Wilcoxon signed-rank, two-sided, over the 12 business means: p = 0.00049** (2 of 4,096 sign patterns; the smallest p possible with 12 businesses). The primary test passes.

**Secondary (pre-registered, business level).** Plain questions right (judge 1): 60.7% with the brain, 10.5% without; business-level difference +0.50, 12 of 12 positive, p = 0.00049; judge 2 identical when pooled (the judges differed on 2 of 438 grades). Owner-catchable mistakes per answer: 3.7 with the brain, 7.6 without; fewer on all 12 businesses, p = 0.00049. Judge preference: brain answer preferred on all 12 businesses (pairs: 58 brain, 8 no brain, 7 ties), p = 0.00049. Fact capture (reported, not tested): 34 of 80 owner facts (42.5%; per-business mean 41.9%, lowest 16.7%) landed in the brain from a question the tool asked about that fact; all 80 were in the brains, the rest from the owner's answer to the tool's closing question.

**Described (not tested).** By model: Claude Opus 5.5 7.69 to 14.67 (+6.98); GPT-5.6-Luna 4.62 to 12.58 (+7.96); Claude Haiku 4.5 3.48 to 3.60 (+0.13; it read the notes tab in 5 of its 24 brain trials and got no plain question right in either condition); Sonnet 5 one pair (+8.5). Each judge alone: +5.42 and +4.68, both 12 of 12. Judge agreement: Spearman 0.91 on scores, 99.5% on plain-question grades, 82% on preference. Blinding: the brain was answer A in 38 pairs and B in 35, with no position bias; 25 of 73 brain answers named the notes tab (0 of 73 without it), so a judge could tell the condition in about a third of pairs.

**Sensitivity (run by the independent verifier, not pre-registered).** Leaving out every pair whose answer names a notes tab: +3.90, 12 of 12, p = 0.00049 (11 businesses, p = 0.00098, when a business left with fewer than half its pairs is dropped). Leaving out each model in turn: +3.6 to +7.5, 12 of 12 each time. Plain questions graded mechanically against the key numbers instead of by the judges (18 questions with a single-number key, all 12 businesses): 67.9% with the brain, 25.7% without, p = 0.0033.

**Verification.** An independent pass recomputed every reported number from the raw trial and verdict files with its own code (not the project's `analyze.py`): all match, the bootstrap interval within seed noise. A second model family (OpenAI GPT-6-Astra), given only the per-pair judge totals, reproduced the primary result exactly. A separate integrity audit confirmed the freeze (46 of 46 tool hashes, 12 of 12 procedure hashes, the tarball hash, the plan body unchanged), that every business, brain, trial and verdict was created after the freeze, that no clean trial read another trial's files, a brief, a key or a judge folder, that every brain copy holds its source data unchanged, and that no brain's owner notes simply state a key answer. Its corrections are deviations 1, 2 and 4, and deviation 3 made prominent. Reports and code: `evals/rigor-confirmatory/verify/`.

**What the result does and does not show.** It shows that, across 12 businesses nobody had seen, handing an AI the same workbook with a brain built by the tool roughly doubled the judged quality of its answer and raised exact answers from about 1 in 10 to about 6 in 10, for the two stronger models tested; the smallest model tested gained nothing. It does not show that a real owner would say as much: the owner agents answered from a written brief and typed every fact they knew into the tool's closing question, so the brains hold more owner knowledge than a busy owner might give, and only 42.5% of it came from the tool's targeted questions. It does not cover Sonnet 5, which the isolation rule removed. All businesses are synthetic and all judges are AI models.
